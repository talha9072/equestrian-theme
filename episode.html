After a hard season of near-misses, a fall at the county show could have broken her. Instead it became the moment everything changed — for her, and for me in the stands.

There's a particular silence in a lorry park after a bad day. Not an empty silence — a full one, heavy with everything nobody's saying. We sat in that silence for twenty minutes before either of us spoke.

She was eleven. We'd driven two hours for the county show, the one she'd circled on the calendar in March. She'd ridden brilliantly all week at home. And then, three strides from the last fence, it all came apart — a stumble, a green flash, and my daughter on the floor of an arena while a hundred strangers held their breath.

She wasn't hurt. Not really. But something had broken anyway, and it took us both a long time to understand what.

What this episode is about

This is an honest conversation about losing — and about the quiet, corrosive pressure we put on our kids without ever meaning to. We talk about the difference between supporting a child and needing them to win, and how I got that line wrong more often than I'd like to admit.

  • Why the result was never actually the point — and how long it took me to believe that
  • The thing she said in the lorry that I've never forgotten
  • How a fall changes your relationship with fear, and what helped her ride again
  • Practical ways we learned to talk about disappointment without rushing to fix it

A note from the Dad

If you're a parent listening to this with a knot in your stomach, recognising yourself in the bits I'm not proud of — you're not alone, and it's not too late. The yard taught my daughter resilience. It taught me to get out of the way. Both lessons took a fall to learn.

— Dad
